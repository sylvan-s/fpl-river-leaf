Where the points come from

A human-in-the-loop risk read, not an optimiser constraint — see docs/adr/0001-xi-scoring-route-composition-chart.md. xP_adj blends goals, assists, clean sheets, defensive actions and bonus into one number per player; this unblends the XI's 49.6 xP/GW back into the six routes that produce it, start-weighted the same way, so the segments below sum to that figure exactly.

Bonus shrinkage constant this build: k=8.3, fitted from observed variance rather than a fallback/clamp value — no flag needed on the segment below.
Composition only, not a risk measure — showing where the points come from doesn't yet say how sure the model is about each route. Goal Involvement and Defensive Contribution carry a Gamma-Poisson dispersion estimate, Clean Sheets a nonlinear transform of Poisson uncertainty, and Bonus a differently-derived shrinkage constant that can itself go unvalidated (above) — three incompatible uncertainty models, not one confidence scale yet. Per-route confidence intervals are deferred to a future gated roadmap item rather than faked here.

PosBuy forThe trap
GK Undisputed #1 first — everything else is void if he's benched. Then low season xGC and save volume, a distant second. Strong save stats on a keeper one bad week from losing the gloves. Starts is the gate for a reason.
DEF Both legs at once — a side with genuinely low xGC / a real clean sheet record, and the player's own CBIT clearing the 10+ per-match threshold reliably (his real hit-rate, not a season average sitting near the line). Either leg alone. A great individual defender on a leaky side rarely banks the clean sheet. High CBIT on a leaky side is often a player under siege, not a good defender.
MID Attacking output first (xGI/90 — a goal pays 5, an assist 3, near forward money) plus the clean sheet sitting on top for free if his side is tight at the back too. A rarer, lower-reliability route exists — a genuinely defensive midfielder clearing 12+ CBIRT — but it's not worth building a pick around.
FWD Pure goals + assists + minutes. No clean sheet credit, and no real defensive-contribution route either. Reading a forward's CBIT/90 as if it predicts anything — 2025/26 data shows forwards essentially never clear 10+ CBIT in a match (0% hit-rate across every matched forward in the pool), so there's no variance left to explain.
What holding the no-Haaland preference actually requires — it is not free to execute, even though it is priced as near-free above.
  • Captaincy has to be reviewed every week, not set once. A Haaland squad has a standing captain and the decision is mostly "anyone beating him this week"; without a nailed 75%-owned explosive premium, the armband goes to whoever's fixture and form line up that gameweek — see captaincy_odds and the escalation check each week, not a default name.
  • Triple Captain is structurally weaker. TEAM_CHANGE_LOG.md already flags this: most managers triple the nailed premium; the realistic targets here (Thiago, B.Fernandes) are good, not explosive. TC is purely a P(haul) maximisation, so this preference caps its ceiling — a real cost the table above does not capture in xP/90 terms.
  • The budget freed up buys flexibility, not just depth. No single £15.5m anchor means more of the squad can turn over as form and fixtures shift, and a genuinely emerging player (a breakout midfielder, a new-signing forward finding his feet) can be worked in without first funding him by selling a name that's still producing. A Haaland squad effectively locks 15%+ of budget for the season; this one doesn't lock any of it.
  • The trade-off is real, not just upside. Spreading value across the XI raises the floor and lowers variance — fewer explosive weeks, fewer disaster weeks. Whether that trade is right depends on where in the mini-league table this squad needs to be climbing from; it is a strategy choice, not a free lunch the model found.
